Price Gap · GE Profile Opal 2.0 Ultra

Same white, same 38 lbs a day. One listing costs $50 more.

Two Amazon listings for the Stone White GE Profile Opal 2.0 Ultra state the same daily output and the same WiFi connectivity, and yet one rings up at $343.00 and the other at $393.00. A third listing, identical in every stated spec but finished in Black, happens to cost exactly as much as the pricier white one. None of that is a coincidence once you read all three titles line by line.

If you search “GE Profile Opal 2.0 Ultra Stone White” on Amazon right now, you won’t land on one listing — you’ll land on two. Both carry the Opal 2.0 Ultra name. Both are finished in the same Stone White. Both state a daily output of 38 lbs. One of them is priced at $343.00. The other is priced at $393.00. That’s a $50.00 gap, roughly 15 percent, between two listings a shopper scanning search results could easily assume are the same purchase.

There’s also a third listing worth putting on the table: the same Opal 2.0 Ultra, same stated spec sheet, finished in Black instead of white, priced at $393.00 — the exact dollar figure of the pricier Stone White listing, to the cent. That third price point is the clearest evidence in this whole comparison that color isn’t what’s driving the gap. We read all three product titles exactly as published, line by line, and what moves between the $343.00 listing and its two $393.00 siblings has nothing to do with Stone White versus Black. We don’t run a physical testing lab and haven’t plugged in any of these three units ourselves; everything below comes from what each listing states in its own name, price and specification, and nothing we can’t point to directly. If you’re also trying to make sense of GE’s wider Opal Ultra naming, our separate look at the original Opal Ultra against the 2.0 Ultra generation covers that broader question first.

The headline numbers

“38 lbs a day” and “WiFi” appear on both — worded two different ways

Start with what’s identical, because it’s the number both titles lead with. The $343.00 Stone White listing states “38 lbs in 24 Hours.” The $393.00 Stone White listing states “38 lbs/day.” That’s the same figure, worded two different ways, and neither title claims a higher output ceiling than the other. If the extra $50.00 bought more ice per day, the titles themselves don’t say so.

Connectivity shows a similar pattern with a small but real wording difference. The $343.00 listing states “WiFi & Smart Connected.” Both $393.00 listings — the pricier Stone White and the Black colorway — state “WiFi & App Control.” Neither listing spells out what, if anything, separates “Smart Connected” from “App Control” in practice; these read like marketing phrasing attached to the same underlying GE Profile app, and we won’t assert a functional gap the titles don’t describe themselves. What we can say plainly is that the two $393.00 listings use identical connectivity wording to each other, while the $343.00 listing uses a different phrase — one more data point suggesting the $393.00 pair are the same product line, and the $343.00 listing is a distinct one.

The ice itself is described two ways too: the $343.00 listing calls it a “Pellet Ice Machine,” while both $393.00 listings reference “Soft Pebble Ice.” Pellet and pebble are the same soft, chewable nugget-style ice this entire category is built around — GE isn’t describing two different ice textures, just two different words for the same product category across listings that were evidently written by different people or at different times.

Same box, different words

‘Scale Inhibiting Filter’ versus ‘Descaling Powder’: the accessory line that actually differs

Here’s where the two price points genuinely separate on paper. The $343.00 listing states its included accessories as a Scale Inhibiting Filter and “Scoop Included” — two items, named plainly, no mention of descaling powder anywhere in the title. Both $393.00 listings state their accessories as “Filter, Descaling Powder & Scoop Included” — three items, with descaling powder called out by name as its own bundled component rather than folded into the filter.

A scale-inhibiting filter and a tub of descaling powder commonly ship together on machines in this category, and it’s entirely possible the $343.00 listing’s seller simply wrote a shorter title that omits an accessory the box still contains. We can’t confirm that from the title alone, so we’re reporting the wording gap rather than assuming the contents match. What we can say with confidence is that descaling powder, named explicitly, is the one concrete accessory difference stated across these three listings — and it tracks with the same $50.00 split that shows up in the daily-output wording and the connectivity phrasing. If how a listing should bundle these accessories is a question you want answered in general, our breakdown of whether the Opal side tank accessory is worth $70 on its own walks through how GE prices individual components for this same product family.

What the listing photos show

One listing photo shows no side tank. Both $393.00 listings show one.

Neither $393.00 title uses the words “side tank” as a headline figure the way GE’s original Opal Ultra does, but both do state “.75 Gallon Tank” and list “Side Tank” among their named features, and their listing photos show a second, narrower unit attached to the machine’s side — the detachable reservoir that lets you top up water without pulling the whole appliance forward. The $343.00 listing’s own photo shows a single, self-contained unit with no second attachment visible beside it, and its title doesn’t state a tank size or a side tank at all.

That’s a visible, checkable difference between the listing images, not a guess on our part — and it lines up exactly with what each title does and doesn’t state. A machine without a separate side reservoir still has to hold water somewhere, just in a smaller built-in space rather than a detachable .75-gallon tank; the $343.00 listing simply doesn’t publish that number, so we won’t estimate one. If a side tank specifically, and the fewer-refills convenience that comes with it, is the reason you’re comparing these three listings in the first place, the product photos and the $0.75-gallon figure on the two $393.00 listings are the detail to confirm directly before you buy the cheaper one expecting the same hardware.

GE states a first-batch time of 10 minutes on both $393.00 listings. Neither the $343.00 Stone White listing nor, by extension, any version without the stated side tank publishes a time-to-first-ice figure, so we don’t have a comparable number for it. We won’t apply the 10-minute figure from one listing to a machine whose own title is silent on it.

The tell

The Black 2.0 Ultra costs $393.00 — exactly what the pricier Stone White listing costs

This is the detail that settles the question of whether color has anything to do with the $50.00 gap. The Black GE Profile Opal 2.0 Ultra states the identical specification sheet as the pricier Stone White listing — .75 Gallon Tank, 38 lbs/day, Soft Pebble Ice, First Batch in 10 Min, WiFi & App Control, Side Tank, Filter, Descaling Powder & Scoop Included — and it’s priced at $393.00. Not close to $393.00. Exactly $393.00, matching the higher of the two Stone White listings to the cent.

If Black commanded a premium over white, or a discount, you’d expect to see that reflected somewhere in these three prices. Instead, the two listings that share an identical spec sheet — one white, one black — share an identical price, while the listing that actually costs less is the one with the shorter, differently worded spec sheet, regardless of its color. The honest read is that Stone White and Black are priced the same within this product generation, and the $50.00 gap you’ll find when shopping by color alone is really a gap between two different spec sheets that happen to both be available in white.

The bigger pattern

Why near-duplicate listings coexist on Amazon at different prices

This isn’t unique to GE or to nugget ice makers. Large manufacturers frequently list variations of the same product line as separate product pages rather than folding every option into one listing with a color or configuration dropdown, especially when the listings were created at different times, by different internal teams, or for different retail purposes. Once two listings exist independently, their titles, bullet points and even their stated accessories can drift apart over time even when the underlying hardware is unchanged — one listing gets updated with a new feature callout, the other doesn’t, and a shopper comparing them months later sees what looks like a meaningful difference that’s really just inconsistent documentation.

We mapped the identical pattern within GE’s own Opal Ultra naming in our separate comparison of the original Opal Ultra against two competing Opal 2.0 Ultra listings, where a $56 gap tracked with three stated details one title included and the other omitted. The lesson repeats here with a different pair of ASINs and a $50.00 gap instead: the brand name and the headline daily-output figure tell you less than the specific listing’s own fine print, and two listings that look interchangeable at a glance can differ in exactly the details — a tank size, a descaling accessory, a first-batch time — that decide whether the extra money is worth paying.

None of this means either listing is mislabeled or that a shopper is being deceived. It means the two listings are not proven to be the same purchase, and the only way to know for certain which one matches what you want is to read the specific listing’s own title and specification table rather than assume parity from the product name and the photo.

Before you pay the extra $50

What to check before paying the higher price

If you’re deciding between the two Stone White listings, don’t rely on the product name alone — both say “Opal 2.0 Ultra,” and that name doesn’t distinguish them. Open each listing’s own specification table and confirm three things directly: whether a side tank or tank volume is stated, whether descaling powder is listed among the included accessories, and whether a first-batch time is published. Those are the three concrete facts that track with the $50.00 gap in this comparison, and they’re the fastest way to tell which listing you’re actually looking at if a seller ever relists either one under a slightly different title.

If none of those three details matter to you — you don’t need a tank figure in writing, you’re fine sourcing descaler yourself later, and first-batch time isn’t a deciding factor — the $343.00 listing nominally gets you the same 38-lbs headline for $50.00 less. If you want those three facts committed to in writing before you hand over your card, the $393.00 listing, in either Stone White or Black, is the one that states them.

FAQ

Common questions

Why are there two Stone White GE Profile Opal 2.0 Ultra listings?

They’re separate Amazon listings under the same product name rather than color variants of one listing, and their titles state different things: the $343.00 listing names a Scale Inhibiting Filter and a scoop with no stated tank size, while the $393.00 listing states a .75-gallon side tank, a 10-minute first-batch time and descaling powder by name. The $50.00 gap tracks with those stated differences, though we can’t confirm from the titles alone whether the underlying hardware differs.

Does the color — Stone White versus Black — affect the price?

Not based on what these three listings show. The Black 2.0 Ultra states the identical specification sheet as the pricier Stone White listing and is priced at the same $393.00, to the cent. The $50.00 gap sits between the two different spec sheets, not between the two colors.

Does the cheaper $343.00 listing have a side tank?

Its title doesn’t state one, and its own listing photo shows a single self-contained unit with no second tank attachment visible, unlike the two $393.00 listings, whose photos show a tank attached to the side and whose titles state a .75-gallon side tank. We won’t assert a tank size the listing itself doesn’t publish, so if a side tank is the deciding feature for you, treat the $343.00 listing as unconfirmed on that point.

How can I check before paying the extra $50.00?

Open each listing’s specification table directly and compare three stated details: whether a tank volume or side tank is named, whether descaling powder is listed among the included accessories, and whether a first-batch time is published. Those three facts track with the price gap in this comparison and are a faster, more reliable check than comparing product names or photos alone.
