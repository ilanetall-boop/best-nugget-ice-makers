Buying Guide · GE Profile Opal Accessories

A $70 tank that only fits one Opal, exactly.

GE sells the Opal Ultra's side water tank as a standalone accessory for $70, promising three times the ice before you have to refill. That promise is real, but it is attached to one specific model number, not to "the Opal" as a family of machines. We read what the accessory's own listing states about fit, design and capacity, and set it against what the Opal Ultra and Opal Mini each already ship with.

The listing title does a lot of work in a little space: “GE Profile Opal 2.0 Ultra Side Water Tank Accessory, 0.75 Gal | Compatible with Opal Ice Maker XPIO23SWSS, Allows for 3X More Ice Before Refill, Flip-and-Fill Design, Stainless Steel.” At $70, it reads like an easy upgrade for anyone tired of refilling a nugget ice maker — until you notice that the compatibility clause names one specific model number, not the Opal line as a whole.

That distinction is the entire question this guide answers. GE's Opal name now covers several different machines and listings, from the original Opal Ultra to the top-fill Opal Mini, and they do not all take water the same way. A $70 accessory that bolts cleanly onto one of them can be completely unusable on another, no matter how good the 3X promise sounds in the title. Before you add this to a cart, the two things worth knowing are what you already own and what the accessory's own listing actually commits to.

The headline number

What “3X More Ice Before Refill” Actually Means

“3X more ice before refill” is a capacity claim, not a speed claim. It is not saying the machine makes ice three times faster, and it is not a restated version of a pounds-per-day figure. It is describing how much longer the machine can keep running on stored water before you have to go pour more in — a statement about the tank, not about the ice-making mechanism inside the unit.

The accessory itself is stated at 0.75 gallons on its own listing. Read literally, “3X more ice before refill” means that adding this tank's worth of water lets the machine produce roughly three times as much ice between trips to the sink as it would running on whatever smaller reservoir it started with, since there is simply more standing water on hand for the machine to draw from. What the listing does not state is an hours-until-empty figure, or a batches-per-fill number, so we are not going to convert that multiplier into a specific refill interval in hours — GE doesn't publish one for this accessory, and a multiplier on tank volume is not automatically the same multiplier on time, since how fast a given household burns through ice varies by use. The honest way to read the claim is as a stated capacity multiplier, confirmed by GE on the product itself, not as a guaranteed number of extra hours unattended.

It is also worth noticing that this is the second time a “roughly 3X” ice-before-refill claim shows up in the Opal family. GE's own listing for the original Opal Ultra states that its included 1-gallon side tank produces up to three times more ice before you touch it than a standard model. That is a different claim, attached to a different, larger tank, on a different listing. The two 3X figures are not interchangeable, and neither should be assumed to apply to a machine the claim wasn't written for.

How it attaches

Flip-and-Fill Design, and the One Model Number It Names

“Flip-and-fill” describes the refill motion rather than a full tear-down: the tank is built to tip or flip open on the machine so you can pour water directly in, rather than requiring you to detach the entire tank, carry it to the sink, and reseat it every time. GE pairs that with a stainless steel shell for the accessory, matching the finish of the machines it is meant to sit beside. That is a real, specific design detail named on the listing, and it is consistent with how GE already describes refilling on its larger Opal side tanks elsewhere in the lineup — a tank you interact with in place, not one you fully remove for routine topping up.

The part of the title that matters more than the design language, though, is the compatibility clause: “Compatible with Opal Ice Maker XPIO23SWSS.” That is a specific model number, printed on a specific machine's rating plate, not a family name. The listing's own product name leads with “Opal 2.0 Ultra,” but the actual fit guarantee it makes is to model XPIO23SWSS specifically. Those are two different ways of describing the same compatibility — one by marketing name, one by model number — and the model number is the one that should decide your purchase, since marketing names get reused across machines with different tank designs far more often than model numbers do.

Practically, that means before you buy this accessory, the step that actually matters is checking the model number on your own machine's rating plate against XPIO23SWSS, not just confirming you own something GE calls an “Opal.” We don't have published data confirming this accessory also fits the original Opal Ultra's model number or either of the separate Opal 2.0 Ultra listings we've compared elsewhere, and the listing itself doesn't claim that broader fit — it names one model, and we're not going to extend that guarantee further than GE already has.

If you already own an Ultra

Why the Opal Ultra Already Ships With a Gallon Tank

The original GE Profile Opal Ultra doesn't need this accessory to get a side tank at all. Its own listing states a 1 Gal XL Side Tank included in the box at $499.00 — a full gallon, already larger than the 0.75-gallon accessory sold separately for $70. We covered exactly how that Ultra tank compares with the newer Opal 2.0 Ultra listings' stated .75-gallon tank in our head-to-head on what the 2.0 redesign actually changed, and the short version is that the original Ultra's included tank is the larger of the two GE has stated publicly.

So for someone who already owns the original Opal Ultra, buying this $70 accessory would mean adding a second, smaller tank on top of a first, larger one already included — not acquiring a side tank for the first time. The accessory's own listing names its compatibility to model XPIO23SWSS, not to the original Ultra's model number, and we have no published confirmation that the original Ultra accepts a second tank running alongside its included one, or that GE's hardware even supports two tanks feeding the same unit at once. Nothing in the data we have states that it does. An existing Ultra owner chasing “more ice before refill” already owns the larger of the two tanks GE has published figures for; this accessory is not the obvious next step for that specific owner, and treating it as an upgrade path without confirming the model number first risks spending $70 on a part that may not attach to the machine already on the counter.

If you own a Mini

Why an Opal Mini Owner Can't Just Bolt This On

The GE Profile Opal Mini is a different design from the side-tank Opal Ultra, and the difference is stated directly in its own product name: a Top Fill Water Reservoir. Water goes in from above, into a reservoir built into the top of the unit, while the machine sits in place. There is no side-mounted tank on the Mini's own listing, and no side tank slot or port for one to flip open and attach to in the way the accessory describes.

That is an architectural difference, not a labeling one, and it's the reason a Mini owner can't simply buy this $70 side tank and expect it to click into place. A flip-and-fill side tank needs a side-tank mount and a water line built into that specific point on the chassis; a top-fill machine was engineered around a completely different water path and doesn't have that mounting point to begin with. The accessory's own compatibility clause backs this up by omission: it names model XPIO23SWSS, and nothing in its listing claims fit with the Mini's top-fill design. Buying it for a Mini on the strength of the 3X promise alone, without confirming the model number, is the single most common way this specific $70 purchase goes wrong. Check the rating plate, not the name

Compatibility by Model Number, Since the Listing Doesn't Fit Every Opal

GE now sells multiple listings under names that all start with “Opal,” and we've already mapped how confusingly close two of those listings sit to each other in price and stated specification in our Opal Ultra vs. Opal 2.0 Ultra breakdown. That pattern — one brand name spread across machines with meaningfully different stated specs — isn't unique to GE either; we found the same shape of problem across a different manufacturer's full catalog in our guide to EUHOMY's lineup, mapped by capacity, where the brand name told shoppers almost nothing and the model-specific numbers told them everything.

This $70 accessory is a clean example of why that distinction matters for parts and accessories, not just for the ice makers themselves. Its own listing states compatibility with exactly one model number: XPIO23SWSS. It does not state compatibility with the original Opal Ultra's model number, and it does not state compatibility with the Opal Mini. “Fits the Opal” as a marketing phrase and “fits your Opal” as a mechanical fact are two different claims, and only the second one should get your money. Before ordering, the step that actually protects your $70 is finding the model number printed on your own machine's rating plate — usually on the back or underside of the unit — and checking it against XPIO23SWSS directly, rather than trusting that any machine GE calls an Opal will accept any accessory GE sells under the Opal name.

The free alternative

When $70 Is Worth It, and When a Bigger Jug Does the Same Job

The $70 accessory earns its price in exactly one scenario: you own model XPIO23SWSS, you've confirmed that against the number on your own rating plate, and you specifically want more standing water in reserve so the machine runs longer between trips to the sink. In that case it is a documented part solving a documented problem, with a design — flip-and-fill — built to make the refill itself less of a chore too.

Outside that scenario, the $70 buys you nothing extra. If your actual goal is simply fewer refill trips in general, and you haven't confirmed your model number matches, the free version of the same fix is to stop using a small cup or glass to top up whatever reservoir your machine already has, and start using a larger pitcher or jug instead. The water goes into the same opening either way; a bigger container just means fewer trips per gallon delivered, at zero dollars and zero compatibility risk. That's the same logic that makes us cautious anywhere a small, specific-sounding upgrade promises convenience across a whole product line — see our look at whether an Amazon Renewed discount is worth it for a similar case where the headline savings mattered less than the fine print underneath it. For the side tank accessory, the fine print is the model number, and it is worth five minutes to check before it is worth $70 to find out the hard way.

FAQ

Common questions

What does “flip-and-fill design” mean on this accessory?

It describes the refill motion rather than a full tear-down. The tank is built to flip or tip open in place so you can pour water directly in, instead of detaching the whole tank, carrying it to the sink, and reseating it. GE pairs that with a stainless steel shell to match the finish of the machines it's sold for.

Does the Opal Ultra need this $70 accessory, since it already has a side tank?

No. The original Opal Ultra's own listing already includes a 1-gallon side tank, which is larger than the 0.75-gallon accessory sold separately. Buying this accessory for an Ultra you already own would add a second, smaller tank on top of a first, larger one included in the box — not give you a side tank for the first time. We have no published confirmation that the original Ultra's hardware supports running two tanks at once.

Can I attach this accessory to an Opal Mini?

No, and the accessory's own listing doesn't claim it fits. The Opal Mini is built around a top-fill water reservoir, not a side tank, so there is no side-tank mounting point on the chassis for this accessory to attach to. The listing states compatibility with model XPIO23SWSS specifically, and nothing in its description extends that to the Mini's top-fill design.

Is model XPIO23SWSS the same as the Opal Ultra or Opal Mini?

We don't have data confirming that. The accessory's listing states compatibility with model XPIO23SWSS by that number alone, not by matching it to the original Opal Ultra's or Opal Mini's own listed model identifiers. The only reliable way to confirm a match is to check the model number printed on your own machine's rating plate against XPIO23SWSS directly, rather than assuming it based on the word “Opal” in either product's name.
