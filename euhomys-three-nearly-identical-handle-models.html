That's the setup we're examining here: ASIN B0C32SGKMJ at $106.41, ASIN B0G3Q4Y676 at $110.03, and ASIN B0GXB42S3F at $147.16. We're not guessing at any of this from the photos — every figure below comes from what EUHOMY itself states in the product title, bullets or listing data for each ASIN. Where a spec isn't published for one of the three, we say so rather than assume it matches the others just because the box looks the same.

Finding #1

Same bullet points, three different prices: what that usually signals

Two of these three listings — B0C32SGKMJ and B0GXB42S3F — share essentially the same product title, the same core claim set, and by every indication the same base machine: “Ready in 6 Mins, 34lbs Per Day, Removable Top Cover, Auto-Cleaning, Portable Sonic Ice Maker with Basket and Scoop, for Home/Party/RV/Camping (Black).” That is not a paraphrase on our part. It is the title EUHOMY wrote, repeated across two separate product pages with two separate ASINs, two separate review histories (which start from zero each time), and a $40.75 gap in price — $106.41 versus $147.16, a 38% markup for what the listing copy describes as the identical machine.

When a brand lists what reads as the same product multiple times with different ASINs and different prices, there are a handful of ordinary explanations, and only one of them is a genuine spec difference. Sometimes it's a packaging or color variant that didn't make it into the title. Sometimes it's a different fulfillment channel — one ASIN shipped and sold by EUHOMY directly, another by a third-party seller using EUHOMY's listing content, each pricing independently. Sometimes an older ASIN is simply the one that's accumulated reviews and the seller is charging a premium for that social proof, even though reviews and ratings aren't something we track or report on this site. And sometimes — this is the least charitable read, and we can't confirm or rule it out from the listing alone — it's a newer ASIN created specifically to escape a negative review history on the old one, relisted at a higher price because the seller is betting shoppers won't comparison-shop by ASIN.

We can't tell you which of those explains the $40 gap between B0C32SGKMJ and B0GXB42S3F, because EUHOMY's own listing copy doesn't distinguish them. What we can tell you is that identical bullet points at different prices is a pattern worth noticing on any brand, not just this one, and the correct response is never to assume the pricier listing earned its premium. Read the next section before you decide which of the three, if any, is worth the extra money.

Finding #2

34, 38 and 34 lbs a day: the capacity claims line up against nothing

Here is where the three listings actually start to disagree, at least on paper. B0C32SGKMJ states 34lbs Per Day in its own title. B0GXB42S3F, the $147.16 listing with the otherwise-identical title and bullets, states the exact same figure: 34lbs Per Day. B0G3Q4Y676, priced in between the two at $110.03, states a different number: 38lbs Daily Sonic Ice, per its title.

Set those three numbers next to the three prices and the ordering stops making sense. The cheapest of the three, B0C32SGKMJ at $106.41, claims 34 lbs a day. The middle-priced one, B0G3Q4Y676 at $110.03, claims the highest output of the group at 38 lbs. The most expensive, B0GXB42S3F at $147.16, claims the same 34 lbs as the cheapest one — meaning you'd be paying a 38% premium over the cheapest listing for a stated capacity that is, on paper, identical to it, and lower than the $110.03 model's claim.

We want to be precise about what we can and can't verify here. A daily-output figure on an Amazon listing is a manufacturer claim, not a measured result, and we have no independent way to confirm any of the three numbers against real output in a real kitchen — we don't run a testing lab, and we're not going to pretend a bullet point is a lab result. What we can say with confidence is that the claims as published don't correlate with price at all across these three ASINs. If daily output were driving the price difference, the $147.16 unit would state a higher number than the $106.41 one. It doesn't. It states the same number. That alone should tell you the capacity figure is not what you're paying for when you pick the expensive listing.

Finding #3

The one genuine difference we could find between the three listings

If the bullet points, the tank size and the viewing window are shared across all three, and the capacity claims don't track the price, is there anything that actually separates these listings? One real difference shows up when you line up the dates behind the data we have on file: our most recent price check on B0GXB42S3F, the $147.16 unit, is dated October 28, 2026, while the two cheaper listings, B0C32SGKMJ and B0G3Q4Y676, were both checked more recently, on November 12, 2026. That is not a spec difference, and we're not claiming it explains the full $40 gap. But it does mean the $147.16 figure is the oldest of the three prices we have, and Amazon pricing on this category moves often enough that a two-week-old number is already less trustworthy than one pulled yesterday.

Beyond that timing gap, the only other distinction visible in the listing data itself is the title text on B0G3Q4Y676: it drops the “Ready in 6 Mins” and “Removable Top Cover, Auto-Cleaning” language that appears on the other two, and states “38lbs Daily Sonic Ice” instead. Whether that reflects an actual mechanical difference — a different compressor, a faster cycle, a genuinely higher-output unit — or just a title rewritten by a different copywriter on the same hardware, we cannot tell from the listing alone. EUHOMY doesn't publish a spec sheet detailed enough to confirm or rule out either explanation, and we're not going to invent one. What we can say honestly: of the three, B0G3Q4Y676 is the only one whose title claims something the other two don't, and it is not the most expensive of the three.

Finding #4

Why the cheapest of the three is not automatically the worst buy

The instinct with three near-identical listings at three different prices is to assume the expensive one must be better, because why else would it cost more. Walk through what we've actually found and that assumption doesn't hold up. B0GXB42S3F at $147.16 shares the exact same title, bullets, 1.2-quart tank and 34 lbs/day claim as B0C32SGKMJ at $106.41. On every spec EUHOMY publishes for either ASIN, they read as the same machine. The $40.75 difference between them is not explained by anything in the product data — it is explained by which ASIN you happen to click.

That makes B0C32SGKMJ, the cheapest of the three, the one with the best-supported claim-to-price ratio: the same stated capacity, the same tank, the same window, for 28% less than the identically-described alternative. It is not automatically the worst buy just because it's the cheapest — in this specific case, cheapest and best-documented-value happen to be the same listing. The lesson generalizes past these three ASINs: when a brand's own listing copy doesn't distinguish two products, the burden is on the pricier one to justify itself, not on the cheaper one to prove it isn't a corner-cut version. Here, EUHOMY's own words don't make that case for the $147.16 unit.

Before you pay more

What to actually check before paying more for the identical machine

This EUHOMY trio isn't a one-off. Near-identical listings at different prices, from the same or different brands, are common enough across this category that it's worth having a short routine rather than trusting the price tag as a quality signal. Before you pay more for what looks like the same nugget ice maker:

1. Copy the title into a text file and diff it against the cheaper listing. If the wording matches beyond the price and the ASIN, that's the strongest single signal that you're looking at the same base product. It's exactly how we found the overlap between B0C32SGKMJ and B0GXB42S3F here.

2. Compare the stated capacity, tank size and included accessories line by line. Don't skim — a single differing word (“38lbs” buried in an otherwise identical sentence) is often the only clue that two listings aren't actually twins, and it's easy to miss if you're reading for vibe rather than specifics.

3. Check how recently the price was verified, if that information is available to you. The oldest of our three EUHOMY price checks was also the most expensive listing; a price that hasn't been refreshed in two weeks on a category that moves daily is not a number to anchor a decision on.

4. Don't assume the newer or pricier ASIN inherited the older one's reputation. A fresh ASIN starts with zero reviews regardless of price, and a listing that costs more while sharing identical copy with a cheaper one has not demonstrated anything beyond a higher price tag.

5. If nothing in the published specs explains the gap, default to the cheaper ASIN. The burden of proof sits with whichever listing costs more. If EUHOMY, or any brand, wanted you to pay extra for something real, that something would show up in the bullets. Here, on two of the three ASINs, it doesn't.

FAQ

Common questions

Are these actually three different machines, or the same one relisted?

We can't say with certainty from the listing data alone, and that's the honest answer. Two of the three ASINs, B0C32SGKMJ and B0GXB42S3F, share word-for-word titles and bullet claims, which strongly suggests the same underlying hardware sold under two ASINs at two prices. The third, B0G3Q4Y676, states a different capacity figure in its own title, which could mean a genuine hardware difference or could just mean a different copywriter described the same unit differently. EUHOMY doesn't publish a detailed enough spec sheet for any of the three to rule either explanation out definitively.

Why would the same company sell what looks like the same product at different prices?

Several ordinary reasons exist beyond anything suspicious: different fulfillment or seller accounts pricing independently, an older ASIN carrying a review history that justifies a premium to some buyers, or a packaging variant too minor to make the title. It's also possible, though we can't confirm it from the listing, that a newer ASIN exists specifically to start fresh after a negative review history on an older one. None of these explanations are visible in EUHOMY's bullet points, which is exactly why we flag the pattern rather than assume a benign reason.

Should I always buy the cheapest listing when titles match?

When the published specs genuinely match — same stated capacity, same tank, same included accessories — yes, the cheaper ASIN is the rational choice, because nothing in the data justifies the premium. The exception is when a listing states something concretely different, the way B0G3Q4Y676 claims 38 lbs instead of 34; in that case you're choosing based on an actual stated difference, not just price, even though we can't verify the claim independently.

Does the 1.2-quart tank limit how much ice I can make at once?

It limits how long the machine runs before you need to add water, not how much ice it can eventually produce over a day. All three EUHOMY listings compared here state the same 1.2-quart tank, and like every countertop nugget maker in this category, the ice it produces isn't stored frozen solid — it melts back into the reservoir and gets remade continuously. A smaller tank means more frequent refills, not a hard ceiling on total daily output, though the daily-output figures themselves remain manufacturer claims we can't independently verify.
