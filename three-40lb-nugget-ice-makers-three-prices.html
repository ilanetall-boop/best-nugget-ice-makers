The headline number is identical. What you pay for it is not.

An EUHOMY, a SenCre, and a second SenCre all print the same claim on the box: 40 lbs of nugget ice a day. One of them costs roughly twice what another does, and two of the three share a brand name. We lined up what each listing actually states — price, accessories, and the one feature that explains most of the gap — instead of taking the shared number at face value.

Three listings, one number. The EUHOMY Nugget Ice Maker Countertop states 40 lbs per 24 hours. The SenCre Nugget Ice Maker states 40 lbs per day. The SenCre Nugget Ice Maker with Handle states the same 40 lbs per day. If you shopped by that one figure alone, all three would look interchangeable — and yet the prices run from roughly $200 at the low end to just under $400 at the high end, a spread of more than double between the cheapest and the most expensive. That gap is the entire subject of this page. Two of these three machines carry the same brand name, which makes the comparison sharper than it would be between three strangers: when SenCre sells its own "budget" and its own "flagship" side by side with an identical daily-output claim, the price difference has to be explained by something other than how much ice the machine makes.

At the prices in our database, the EUHOMY runs $199.85, the plain SenCre runs $219.99, and the SenCre with Handle runs $399.99. (This comparison was first framed around a slightly lower entry price near $180 for the EUHOMY; Amazon moves these figures often, and $199.85 is what the listing shows as of our most recent check.) We're not going to pretend the daily-capacity number tells you anything useful about that spread, because on paper it doesn't — it's identical across all three. What does explain it is sitting in plain sight in each product title: what's bundled, what's built in, and whose name is on the badge.

The first question

Why three machines can claim the identical "40 lbs a day"

A daily-output figure on a nugget ice maker listing is a manufacturer's own stated ceiling, not an independently measured result, and as we cover in more depth in how many pounds of ice a day you actually need, it assumes a reservoir that stays topped up for a full 24 hours — a condition almost nobody's kitchen actually meets. Because it's self-reported and because "40 lbs" has become close to a default marketing number across this entire product category, two unrelated brands and two SKUs from the same brand can print the identical figure without the underlying machines needing to be remotely similar in build, speed, or bundled features. The number is cheap to print and expensive to verify, so it drifts toward a round, competitive-looking figure almost regardless of what's actually inside the housing.

That's exactly what happened here. EUHOMY and SenCre are different companies entirely, and nothing requires their 40-lb claims to be comparable beyond both appearing in a product title. More tellingly, SenCre's own $219.99 model and its own $399.99 model carry the identical claim against each other — which means a single company looked at two different machines, at two very different price points, and decided 40 lbs a day was the right headline number for both. If a daily-output claim were a reliable proxy for machine quality or capability, a brand wouldn't need to charge $180 more for the second one. The fact that SenCre does charge that much more, for the same stated ceiling, is the clearest evidence in this comparison that the number on its own isn't doing much work.

The cheapest of the three

The EUHOMY at $199.85: self-cleaning and a scoop, nothing else claimed

The full title is specific about what's included: EUHOMY Nugget Ice Maker Countertop, 40Lbs/24H Sonic Ice Maker Machine, Self-Cleaning Pellet Ice Makers with Ice Scoop&Basket, Countertop Pebble Ice Maker for Home/Kitchen/Office (ASIN B0GVYRDGS4), listed in the 40lbs size variant at $199.85. Strip away the repeated branding — "Sonic Ice," "Pebble Ice," the three rooms it's marketed for — and exactly two functional claims remain: it self-cleans, and it ships with an ice scoop and a basket. That's the entire feature set stated in the data we have for this listing. No handle, no window, no stated tank volume, no timer.

That's not a criticism; it's the honest read of what $199.85 buys here. A self-clean cycle is a real, useful feature on a machine that recirculates the same water continuously — it's the single function that does the most to keep mineral scale from building up without the owner remembering to run a manual descale every few weeks. A scoop and a basket are the kind of thing every nugget ice maker needs eventually anyway; having them included instead of bought separately is a modest but genuine saving. What you're not getting at this price, based on the listing's own claims, is anything beyond those two things. If either self-cleaning or the included accessories is the deciding factor for you, this is the cheapest of the three machines compared here that states both outright.

The middle tier

The SenCre at $219.99: the plainer version of the same brand's own flagship

The title here is deliberately bare: SenCre Nugget Ice Maker 40lbs/Day Countertop Ice Makers Chewable Pellet Ice (ASIN B0HF4QN93Z), priced at $219.99. The listing we have doesn't name a size variant beyond this single SKU, and it doesn't claim a carry handle, a self-clean cycle, or any bundled accessory the way either the EUHOMY above or SenCre's own pricier model below do. The only functional language in the title beyond the daily-output figure is "chewable pellet ice" — a description of the ice itself, not a feature of the machine.

Read next to EUHOMY's listing, that's a slightly odd position: $20.14 more than the EUHOMY, for a title that claims fewer stated features than the EUHOMY's self-clean-plus-accessories bundle. Read next to SenCre's own $399.99 model, which we cover next, the picture sharpens considerably. This $219.99 SKU is SenCre's entry point into its own nugget ice maker line — the version without the one physical feature its sibling adds, at just over half that sibling's price. Nothing in the data we have suggests this machine is missing anything structural; it simply isn't claiming anything extra. If you want a SenCre-branded machine at the lowest price SenCre itself offers one at, and you don't specifically want the feature covered in the next section, this is that machine.

The flagship

The SenCre at $399.99 with a handle: what the extra $180 over its own sibling buys

The full title: SenCre Nugget Ice Maker with Handle 40lbs/Day Countertop Ice Maker Soft Ice (ASIN B0HFJ3QTVK), listed in the 40LBS size variant at $399.99. Subtract SenCre's own $219.99 model from this one and the difference is exactly $180.00 — a clean number, and the one this entire comparison is really about. For that $180, based strictly on what's stated in the two titles, you get a built-in carry handle and the phrase "Soft Ice" in place of the plainer model's "Chewable Pellet Ice." The daily-output figure doesn't move. The size variant label doesn't meaningfully change. The brand doesn't change.

A carry handle is a real, physical feature with a real use: it means the machine is built to be picked up and moved — off a counter and into a pantry, out to a patio, between a kitchen and a bar cart — rather than set down once and left there. Whether that's worth $180 depends entirely on whether you'd actually use it. We don't have a stated weight for either SenCre model, so we can't tell you how much easier the handle makes lifting a machine that's still, by category standards, a full-sized countertop appliance rather than something genuinely portable. "Soft Ice" versus "Chewable Pellet Ice" in the two titles reads as marketing language describing the same basic product category — soft, chewable nugget ice is the entire point of every machine in this comparison — and we won't treat it as a separate functional claim distinct from the handle. On the data available, the handle is the one concrete thing $180 buys here, and anyone paying that premium should be doing it specifically for the ability to move the machine, not for a different kind of ice.

Our method

How we compared — and what we can’t tell you

What we did

Three titles, read line by line

We compared the published product titles, stated daily output and current Amazon prices for the EUHOMY and two SenCre countertop nugget ice makers that each claim 40 lbs a day. Where a title states a feature directly — self-cleaning, included accessories, a carry handle — we report it as stated. Where a spec such as tank volume, housing material or machine weight isn't in the data we have, we say so rather than estimate one.

What we didn’t

We don’t run a testing lab

We do not physically test these machines, we do not own them, and we have not weighed, lifted or timed any of the three. We have no owner reviews or star ratings for these ASINs, so none are cited or implied here. What we can do honestly is take each listing's own claims at face value, set them against each other and against the price difference, and be explicit about which differences are documented and which simply aren't stated anywhere in our data.

FAQ

Common questions

If all three claim 40 lbs a day, why do the prices differ so much?

Because the daily-output figure is a manufacturer's own stated ceiling, not an independently measured result, and it's become close to a default number across this whole product category. The price difference here tracks bundled and physical features instead — self-cleaning and included accessories on the EUHOMY, nothing extra stated on the plainer SenCre, and a built-in carry handle on the pricier SenCre — not a difference in how much ice any of the three is rated to produce.

What does the extra $180 on the handled SenCre actually buy?

Based on the two SenCre listings' own titles, one concrete thing: a built-in carry handle. The daily-output claim, the brand, and the general machine category don't change between the $219.99 model and the $399.99 model. If you'd never move the machine once it's placed, that $180 is paying for a feature you won't use.

Is the plain $219.99 SenCre missing something besides the handle?

Not according to the data we have — its title simply doesn't claim the handle, and it also doesn't claim the self-cleaning cycle or included scoop and basket that the EUHOMY's listing states. We don't have confirmation either way on tank size or build material for any of the three machines in this comparison, so we won't assert a hidden downgrade beyond what each title actually says.

Which of these three is the best value?

By price divided across the identical 40-lb claim, the EUHOMY is the cheapest per stated pound at roughly $5.00, against about $5.50 for the plain SenCre and $10.00 for the handled SenCre. That arithmetic favors the EUHOMY and the plain SenCre for anyone who doesn't specifically need to relocate the machine. The handled SenCre is the better pick only for the buyer who has an actual reason to carry it somewhere.
