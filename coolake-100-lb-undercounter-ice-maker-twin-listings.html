Honest Take · Undercounter Nugget Ice Makers

Same 100 lbs a day, same dual baskets. One listing costs $200 more.

Two Coolake Amazon listings for a 15-inch, 100-lbs-a-day undercounter nugget ice maker with dual ice baskets are priced $1,299.99 and $1,099.99 — a $200.00 gap between what reads as the same machine. A third listing, from a brand called Velieta, claims the identical 100 lbs a day for $389.49, a little under a third of either Coolake price. We read all three listings line by line to find out what, if anything, the extra money actually buys.

Search for a 15-inch undercounter nugget ice maker from Coolake on Amazon and you won’t land on one listing — you’ll land on two. Both are titled as a “15 in Undercounter Nugget Ice Maker Machine” or near enough. Both state 100 lbs/24H. Both state Dual Ice Baskets. Both describe a freestanding, chewable-pellet sonic ice machine with an LCD control panel, a 5-15 minute first batch, and a 24-hour timer. One of these listings (ASIN B0HMGYCW57) is priced at $1,299.99. The other (ASIN B0HJG934WJ) is priced at $1,099.99. That’s a $200.00 difference, about 15 percent, between two product pages a shopper comparing search results could easily read as identical.

There’s a third machine worth putting on the table before anyone decides whether that $200.00 gap is worth worrying about. A listing from a brand called Velieta (ASIN B0HJGRBBV1) states the same 100 lbs daily figure, in the same under-counter, self-clean, freestanding format — priced at $389.49, under a third of either Coolake price. We don’t run a physical testing lab and haven’t plumbed any of these three machines into a cabinet ourselves. Everything below comes from what each listing states in its own title and nothing we can’t point to directly.

The two Coolake listings

“100 lbs/24H with Dual Ice Baskets” appears on both, word for word

Read the two Coolake titles side by side and the overlap is almost total. The $1,299.99 listing (B0HMGYCW57) is titled: “Coolake 15 in Undercounter Nugget Ice Maker Machine 100 lbs/24H with Dual Ice Baskets, Freestanding Chewable Pellet Sonic Ice Machine with LCD Control, 5-15 Mins Ice Ready, 24H Timer for Home Bar.” The $1,099.99 listing (B0HJG934WJ) is titled: “Coolake 15″ Undercounter Nugget Ice Maker 100 lbs/24H with Dual Ice Baskets | Freestanding Chewable Pellet Sonic Ice Machine with smart LCD control, 5-15 Mins Ice Ready, 24H Timer for Home Bar Hotel.”

Lay those two strings over each other and exactly two things don’t match: the cheaper listing adds “smart” in front of “LCD control” (the pricier one just says “LCD Control”), and the cheaper listing adds “Hotel” to the intended-use line, reading “for Home Bar Hotel” instead of “for Home Bar.” Everything else — the 15-inch width, the 100 lbs/24H figure, the dual baskets, the freestanding-and-undercounter framing, the sonic mechanism, the 5-15 minute first batch, the 24-hour timer — is stated identically, in the same order, on both.

Amazon’s own variant labels add one more wrinkle. The pricier listing’s size/variant selector is named 100LBS/24H-Nugget; the cheaper one’s is named 100LBS/Day — two different strings for what both titles already state is the same output, not a second capacity hiding behind a dropdown.

What's checkable

A ‘smart’ qualifier and the word ‘Hotel’ — that's the entire stated gap

Neither of the two differences we found changes what the machine does on paper. “Smart LCD control” versus plain “LCD Control” is the kind of adjective a listing copywriter adds or drops between drafts; neither title describes a distinct control scheme, a WiFi feature, an app, or any capability the other doesn’t also claim. Both listings specify the same screen-based control panel with no app connectivity stated on either page, so “smart” here reads as marketing language rather than a documented feature — and we won’t assert a functional gap that only one adjective, unsupported by any other spec, implies.

Adding “Hotel” to the intended-use line is a keyword-targeting choice, not a hardware claim. A machine marketed “for Home Bar” and the identical machine marketed “for Home Bar Hotel” are not two different duty cycles; neither title states a commercial-use rating or any spec that would separate a home installation from a hospitality one. It reads as an attempt to catch a second category of search traffic on an otherwise identical page.

That leaves the $200.00 gap between these two Coolake listings unexplained by anything either title states. It’s possible the two ASINs were created at different times by the same seller and have simply drifted apart in price since; it’s also possible one is a newer production run with an undisclosed change neither title mentions. We can’t confirm either explanation from the listing text, so we’re reporting the gap as unexplained rather than guessing at a reason for it.

The third listing

Velieta claims the same 100 lbs for $389.49 — under a third of either Coolake price

The Velieta listing (B0HJGRBBV1) is titled: “100 lbs Nugget Ice Maker Under Counter Self-Clean Compact Freestanding | Nugget ice machine, Sonic ice maker, Chewable pebble ice, LED alerts, Auto-stop, Steel, Filter, Home, Cafe, Office, Bar.” The headline figure matches both Coolake listings exactly, and the broad framing matches too: under-counter and freestanding, sonic ice-making technology, chewable pellet-style ice (called “pebble” here, the same soft nugget ice this site is built around, under a different word).

At $389.49, Velieta is priced at roughly 35 percent of the $1,099.99 Coolake listing and roughly 30 percent of the $1,299.99 one — comfortably under a third of either. That’s the number worth sitting with before anything else in this comparison: three listings claiming the same daily output, with a gap between the cheapest and the most expensive of roughly $910.50.

What Velieta’s title does not state is just as relevant as what it does. Neither “Dual” nor “baskets” appears anywhere in it, there’s no stated width (Coolake states 15 inches on both of its listings, a figure that matters directly for an undercounter cutout — see our separate guide on what else an undercounter installation actually asks of a machine), and there’s no stated first-batch time or timer duration. In exchange, Velieta names three things neither Coolake title mentions at all: LED alerts, an Auto-stop function, and a Filter. We’re reporting those as a different checklist, not a worse or better one — each brand named the features it chose to lead with, and the two lists don’t overlap much beyond the 100-lbs headline and the under-counter format.

The bigger question

A near-duplicate listing isn't automatically a scam — here's when it's worth worrying

Two listings for what reads as the same product, at two different prices, is a common and mostly unremarkable pattern on Amazon, and it isn’t unique to Coolake or to nugget ice makers. Manufacturers and their resellers frequently create a second product page rather than folding every price point into one listing with a variant dropdown, sometimes because a second ASIN was set up for a different promotion or fulfillment arrangement and the two pages never got reconciled afterward. We found and wrote about an almost identical pattern on a GE Profile Opal 2.0 Ultra, where two Stone White listings of the same machine sat $20.99 apart with no stated hardware difference between them. Near-duplicate listings at different prices are, on the evidence of this site’s own research, a pattern worth expecting across this category, not a one-off red flag specific to one brand.

That said, a near-duplicate listing is worth real scrutiny, not a shrug, when a few specific things line up: a seller name that doesn’t match the brand in the title, a “Ships from and sold by” line that routes through an unfamiliar third party rather than the brand or Amazon itself, a price dramatically lower than every other listing for the same spec sheet (not just somewhat lower, the way Velieta is here), or a product image that doesn’t match the stated dimensions. None of those signals can be verified from a title alone, which is the point: open the actual listing page and check the seller information yourself before paying, on any of the three machines here.

What we can say about these three specifically is that nothing in any of the three titles reads as a red flag on its own. Two Coolake ASINs with a cosmetic wording difference and an unexplained price gap is consistent with ordinary listing drift, not deception. A third-party brand at a much lower price for a similar spec sheet is consistent with a genuinely cheaper undercounter machine built to a lighter feature list, which is a legitimate category — see our look at built-in nugget ice makers at very different price points for how wide that spread runs even among machines with nothing hidden.

The real question

What does $710.50 to $910.50 over the Velieta actually buy?

Strip away the two cosmetic wording differences between the Coolake listings and the honest answer is: a documented 15-inch width, dual ice baskets named explicitly, a stated first-batch time, and a 24-hour timer — four things Coolake’s titles commit to in writing that Velieta’s title does not state at all. Whether Velieta simply omitted those details while still including them in the box, or genuinely lacks a second basket or a built-in timer, isn’t something a title can answer. We won’t assume parity any more than we’ll assume a gap — it’s a real unknown, and the first thing to check on Velieta’s own page before treating the price difference as free money.

Two ice baskets rather than one is the single most concrete, checkable difference in Coolake’s favor, because it addresses something every nugget ice maker in this category shares: the bin isn’t a freezer, and ice melts back into the reservoir and gets remade continuously rather than sitting in frozen storage. A second basket, if it genuinely rotates in while the first is drained or cleaned, is a real answer to that problem rather than a marketing phrase — it just isn’t one Velieta’s title claims to match or compete with. Our separate breakdown of why daily output and bin or basket capacity are different numbers entirely goes into why a stated capacity detail is worth more than it looks on a spec sheet.

What you’re not documented as buying, on this evidence, is a faster machine, a bigger daily output, or a more capable control system. The first-batch window and the daily figure are identical on both Coolake listings regardless of price, and “smart LCD control” doesn’t describe anything Velieta’s plain feature list couldn’t also be doing under different words. The honest framing: the $200.00 gap between the two Coolake listings buys nothing checkable, and the larger gap to Velieta buys a handful of named features set against Velieta’s own named features of LED alerts, auto-stop and a filter, which Coolake doesn’t claim either.

FAQ

Common questions

Why does Coolake have two nearly identical listings for the same ice maker?

Both listings state the same 15-inch width, 100 lbs/24H output, dual ice baskets, first-batch time and 24-hour timer. The only wording differences are a “smart” qualifier added to “LCD control” on the cheaper listing and the word “Hotel” added to its intended-use line — neither describes a documented feature. The $200.00 price gap between them isn't explained by anything either title states; it's consistent with two ASINs created at different times that have simply drifted apart in price.

Is paying $200.00 more for the pricier Coolake listing worth it?

Based on the published titles, there's no stated reason to. Every spec on the $1,299.99 listing also appears on the $1,099.99 one. If you've decided Coolake's documented width, dual baskets and timer are worth the brand premium over Velieta, the cheaper of the two Coolake listings gets you the identical spec sheet for $200.00 less.

Is the $389.49 Velieta the same machine as the Coolake listings?

We can't confirm that, and we won't assume it. Velieta's title matches the 100-lbs daily figure and the under-counter, freestanding format, but it doesn't state a width, a basket count, or a first-batch time the way both Coolake listings do. It does name LED alerts, an auto-stop function and a filter, none of which Coolake's titles mention. Treat the two as different stated feature lists rather than the same product at a lower price.

Is a near-duplicate Amazon listing a sign of a scam?

Not by itself. Large brands and their resellers frequently run more than one listing for close variations of the same product, and prices on near-identical listings can drift apart over time without anything deceptive happening. What's worth checking directly on the listing page is the seller name, whether it's fulfilled by the brand or a third party, and whether the product images match the stated specs — not the price gap on its own.
