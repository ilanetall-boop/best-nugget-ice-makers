Open the nugget ice maker category on Amazon and sort by anything — price, output, reviews — and the same two brand names surface over and over, interleaved with a rotating cast of names that look suspiciously similar and vanish within a season. The shelf

Why two brands fill most of the budget shelf

Countertop nugget ice makers are a relatively new consumer category, built almost entirely around one mechanism — an auger that compacts flaked ice into the soft, chewable pellets the whole category is named for. That mechanism isn't cheap to engineer from nothing, and in a category this size, it's far more economical for a brand to license or source a proven auger-and-compressor assembly than to design one in-house. Silonn and EUHOMY were both early and aggressive about putting that mechanism into a consumer-facing countertop box at a price under $300, well below what GE charges for its Opal line, and both now have enough SKUs on Amazon — compact versions, handle versions, tall versions, 44-lbs versions — that between the two of them they occupy most of what a shopper sees on the first page of results for "nugget ice maker."

That scale is also why they're the two names worth comparing directly rather than lumping in with the smaller brands. A buyer choosing between Silonn and EUHOMY is choosing between the two companies that have shipped enough units, across enough listings, to be treated as the established options in this price bracket — not the newest brand to show up with a near-identical photo set. Whether that scale translates into a better machine, rather than just more listings, is the actual question this comparison is trying to answer.

Brand #1

The Silonn flagship: a 3-litre tank and a 12-hour timer

Silonn's flagship in this comparison is its 44 lbs/24H Nugget Ice Maker with a 3-litre removable tank and a 12-hour timer, priced at $279.98. The headline capacity figure — 44 lbs of ice in 24 hours — is the same number EUHOMY prints on its own flagship, so it isn't the number that separates the two. What Silonn is actually selling at this price is the tank behind that figure: a full 3 litres of water capacity, removable as a single unit for filling at the sink or wiping out between uses, rather than a reservoir you top up through a narrow opening while the machine stays in place.

The second number on the listing that matters is the 12-hour timer. A timer function on a nugget ice maker is a scheduling control, not a bigger tank by itself, but paired with 3 litres of water it changes what the machine is actually built for: longer unattended stretches between the moment you fill it and the moment you need to come back. That's a meaningfully different design target than a machine optimized to produce its first batch as quickly as possible, which is the trade EUHOMY makes with its own flagship below. We don't have a published water-tank capacity for every machine in this category stated with this level of specificity, which is exactly why the 3-litre figure on Silonn's listing is worth taking at face value rather than rounding down to "about the same as everyone else."

What Silonn's listing does not give us is a stated time-to-first-ice figure for this specific model, the way EUHOMY and Kismile do in their own product titles. If how quickly the first batch arrives after you plug it in matters more to you than how long it runs unattended afterward, that's a detail to confirm on the live listing rather than assume from the tank size alone.

Brand #2

The EUHOMY flagship: speed to first batch over tank size

EUHOMY's flagship here is the 44lbs/24H Nugget Ice Maker Countertop, Ready in 7–10 Minutes, in black, priced at $199.95 — roughly $78 less than the Silonn, for the same stated 44-lbs daily figure. The detail EUHOMY chose to put directly in the product title, rather than bury in a specifications table, is time to first batch: 7 to 10 minutes. That's a deliberate signal about what the company thinks matters to a buyer at this price point, and it's the opposite emphasis from Silonn's tank-and-timer pitch. Where Silonn is selling fewer interruptions over a long stretch, EUHOMY is selling the shortest possible wait between plugging the machine in and pouring your first glass.

We don't have a stated water-tank volume for this specific EUHOMY listing in the data we're working from here, and we're not going to borrow a number from a different EUHOMY SKU or guess one from a product photo — that's exactly the kind of unverifiable spec this site won't assert. What the listing does commit to, in writing, is the 44-lbs daily output, the 7–10 minute time to first ice, and the price. If tank size specifically is what decides this for you, treat that as an open question to check on the live listing, not a gap this article is going to fill with a guess.

That absence is also, in its own way, informative. A company that leads with speed and daily output rather than reservoir size is telling you, implicitly, where it expects you to notice the difference: at the start of a session, not three hours into one. Whether that's the right trade for your kitchen depends entirely on whether you run this machine in short bursts or long unattended stretches — which is the same question Silonn's listing answers from the other direction.

The undercut

Where a third, cheaper brand undercuts both on paper

Add a third name to this comparison and the picture gets less tidy, in a useful way. The Kismile Nugget Ice Maker Countertop, 35 lbs/Day, Stainless Steel lists at $144.98 — about $55 below EUHOMY's flagship and $133 below Silonn's, and it says so plainly in its own title that it's built with a stainless-steel body, a detail neither of the two flagship listings above states as explicitly for their own exteriors. On paper, the gap in daily output is smaller than the gap in price: Kismile's 35 lbs/day is about 20% below the 44 lbs both category leaders claim, for roughly half the cost of the Silonn and nearly 30% less than the EUHOMY.

That arithmetic is exactly why "on paper" is doing real work in this section's heading. A lower daily-output number next to a much lower price looks, at a glance, like the better deal of the three. What the Kismile listing doesn't give us is a stated tank volume, a time-to-first-ice figure, or a timer function — the three specifics that separate Silonn's and EUHOMY's pitches from each other. Without those numbers, we can't tell you whether Kismile reaches 35 lbs/day through a fast cycle and a small tank, a slow cycle and a big one, or something in between. The honest reading of a cheaper listing with fewer published numbers isn't "it's hiding something bad." It's that the company spent its title on price and material instead of on the two specs that would let you compare it apples-to-apples against the category leaders, and that omission is the actual price of the $55 to $133 you save.

The open secret

What “from the same factory” probably means, and why it doesn’t make them identical

Anyone who has scrolled through enough nugget ice maker listings has noticed it: control panels with the same button layout, bodies with the same rounded corners, spec sheets that quote the same 33, 35, 36 or 44 lbs/day figures across brands that have never heard of each other. This is a well-known pattern in commodity countertop-appliance categories generally, not a claim specific to any one of these three products: when a mechanism is expensive to design but cheap to license, it's common for several market-facing brands to build their listings around hardware sourced from a small number of contract manufacturers, each brand then adding its own housing, firmware, controls and customer support on top.

We have no supply-chain documentation for Silonn, EUHOMY or Kismile specifically, and we're not asserting that these three particular ASINs came off the same production line — that's not a claim we can verify, and we won't pretend otherwise. What we can say, as general knowledge about how this category tends to work, is that shared manufacturing at the mechanism level is common enough in this bracket that visual similarity between two brands' machines doesn't by itself tell you anything about which one to buy.

Here's why it also doesn't make them identical, even where the underlying hardware overlaps: the tank, the timer function, the firmware controlling the cycle, the warranty terms, the customer-service line you call when something goes wrong, and the quality control at the point of final assembly are all decisions an individual brand makes on top of whatever base mechanism it's working from. That's precisely the gap this comparison keeps running into — Silonn commits to a 3-litre tank and a 12-hour timer in writing; EUHOMY commits to a 7–10 minute first batch in writing; Kismile commits to a stainless body and a price and leaves the rest of the sheet blank. A shared factory, if that's what's happening here, would explain why the daily-output numbers cluster the way they do. It wouldn't explain, and doesn't excuse, the real differences in what each brand is willing to put in writing about its own machine.

The decision

The pick, by which spec you actually use

There's no single winner here, because Silonn and EUHOMY aren't actually competing on the same spec. They're both printing 44 lbs/24h on the box and then pointing at two different reasons to believe them: Silonn at the tank and the timer, EUHOMY at the clock from plug-in to first batch. Treating this as a single ranked list hides that difference instead of using it.

If the way you'll actually use this machine involves filling it once and walking away — a home bar on a Friday night, an office kitchen that gets used all afternoon, a household that doesn't want to think about the reservoir more than once a day — the 3-litre tank and 12-hour timer on the Silonn is the spec that matches that behavior, and the extra $78 over EUHOMY is the price of that tank, not of a better machine in the abstract. If instead you're making ice in shorter sessions and the thing that would actually annoy you is standing around waiting for the first batch after you've just filled it, EUHOMY's stated 7–10 minutes is the number that matches that use case, at the lower of the two flagship prices. And if you're not yet sure this category is for you at all, or you need output closer to 35 lbs than 44 and want to spend the least money finding that out, the Kismile at $144.98 is the honest way to test the premise before committing to either flagship's higher price.

What we wouldn't do is pick the cheapest machine and assume it performs identically to the two leaders just because the daily-output numbers are in the same neighborhood, or pick the most expensive one assuming "most expensive" automatically means "best built." Match the spec to the habit, not the price tag to the brand name. FAQ

Common questions

Is Silonn or EUHOMY the better brand overall?

Neither listing supports a blanket answer, because the two flagships compared here aren't competing on the same spec. Silonn's 44-lbs machine commits in writing to a 3-litre removable tank and a 12-hour timer; EUHOMY's 44-lbs machine commits to a 7–10 minute time to first batch. One is built around fewer interruptions over a long stretch, the other around getting to your first glass fastest. Which brand is "better" depends entirely on which of those two things you'd actually notice in your own kitchen.

Are Silonn and EUHOMY machines made in the same factory?

We have no supply-chain documentation for either brand and aren't asserting that these specific machines share a production line. What is well known in commodity countertop-appliance categories generally is that several market-facing brands often build on hardware sourced from a small number of contract manufacturers, which is a common and reasonable explanation for why daily-output figures and control layouts across brands in this category can look so similar. It doesn't tell you anything about which brand's tank, timer, firmware or customer support is actually better — those are decisions each brand makes on top of whatever it's building from.

Why is the Kismile so much cheaper if the daily output is close?

Partly the roughly 20% lower output figure itself (35 lbs/day against 44), and partly that Kismile's listing simply publishes fewer of the specifics — no stated tank volume, timer, or time-to-first-ice — that Silonn and EUHOMY both commit to for their flagships. A lower price next to fewer published specs isn't evidence of a worse machine on its own, but it does mean you're buying with less information about how it behaves once it's running, which is the real trade for the lower cost.

Does the 12-hour timer on the Silonn actually matter?

It matters specifically if you want to schedule when the machine runs rather than starting it manually every time, and it pairs with the 3-litre tank to support longer unattended operation. It's a scheduling feature, not extra capacity by itself — the tank size is still what determines how long the machine can run before it needs more water. If you'd use the machine in short, manual sessions rather than scheduled unattended ones, the timer is a feature you likely won't lean on, and EUHOMY's faster time-to-first-ice may matter more to your actual routine.
